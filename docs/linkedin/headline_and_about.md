About section
The bottleneck in biomedical AI is not the models. It is the data upstream of them — fragmented, poorly governed, and modelled by teams where the domain knowledge and the engineering capability are in different people.

I am building Synapse Data to close that gap.

My background sits at an unusual intersection: a PhD in Life Sciences (Paris-Saclay), a master's in Biophysics (KU Leuven), and a postgraduate in Applied AI (EhB), combined with hands-on data engineering and product ownership in industry. That combination means I can reason through a clinical data modelling decision with a biostatistician, implement the resulting pipeline correctly, and defend its regulatory traceability to a compliance team — without those being three separate conversations.

In practice, this looks like:

Data products built to modern engineering standards — schema contracts, quality SLAs, lineage, refresh patterns
Clinical standards integration: OMOP CDM, CDISC SDTM, so data products speak the language of clinical data teams from day one
GenAI pipelines for structured data extraction from regulatory and clinical text, designed for GMP environments
Data governance: audit trails, access control, and lineage for clinical research contexts — 21 CFR Part 11, EU AI Act, GDPR as architectural constraints, not compliance checkboxes
As a reference implementation, I am building an open-access genetic disease data mesh for Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy on Databricks: linking mutation profiles from public variant registries to clinical trial eligibility criteria via a governed, versioned data product architecture. The DMD work is both a working product and a reusable template for extension to SMA, Huntington's disease, and the lysosomal storage disorders.

Synapse Data works with large pharma, Series A/B rare disease biotechs, and academic research platforms — embedded, at a scientific depth that a generalist consultancy cannot replicate.

Based in Belgium, at the centre of European pharma and biotech.
